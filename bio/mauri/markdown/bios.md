## Long Bio

Maurizio Ungaro is a nuclear physicist and simulation software developer at
[Jefferson Lab](https://www.jlab.org). His research focuses on the internal structure and dynamics of
the nucleon, including meson electroproduction, nucleon resonances, and the transition between
hadronic and partonic degrees of freedom. His technical work connects detector
operation, Geant4 simulation, CLAS12 production workflows, and scientific
software infrastructure.

He develops and supports [GEMC](https://gemc.github.io/home/), a database-driven Geant4 simulation
framework, and its next-generation GEMC3 framework with Python tools through
[pygemc](https://github.com/gemc/pygemc). Recent development includes CLAS12 geometry and detector-plugin
migration, CAD mesh repair and optimization, and reproducible construction, overlap, and particle-tracking
checks. He also works on interactive detector visualization, including Geant4 XR integration, multilingual
software tools, and portable build and installation workflows.

His production work includes [CLAS12 simulation releases](https://github.com/gemc/clas12Tags) and
[simGrid workflows](/home/osg/osg) using HTCondor and Open Science Grid resources. His detector
responsibilities include operation, maintenance, calibration, and performance studies of the CLAS12
[Low Threshold Cherenkov Counter](/home/ltcc/ltcc). Through
[Geant4 at JLab](https://jeffersonlab.github.io/g4home/), tutorials, examples, and technical notes,
he helps researchers and students use and validate detector simulations.

His earlier and collaborative work includes experiments at [SPring-8](https://www.spring8.or.jp/en/)
in Japan and participation in the [Heavy Photon Search](https://www.jlab.org/physics/hall-b/other)
collaboration.
